
========================================================================
0. DATA
========================================================================
train-part 6,000  validation 1,500  test 2,500

The tokenizer is trained on TRAIN-PART TEXT ONLY. Training it on
validation or test would leak information about words the model is not
supposed to know exist.

training corpus   6,000 tweets, 141,646 words
train types       28,454
test types        15,751
UNSEEN test types 7,676  (48.7% of test types)
unseen occurrences 8,524  (14.3% of test tokens)

Those unseen words are what this whole component exists to rescue.

========================================================================
1. TRAIN TOKENIZERS
========================================================================
SentencePiece learns the pieces from raw text - no rules, no dictionary.
  unigram : probabilistic, keeps pieces that best explain the corpus
  bpe     : greedy, repeatedly merges the most frequent pair

  unigram  requested=1000   actual=1,000
  unigram  requested=2000   actual=2,000
  unigram  requested=4000   actual=4,000
  unigram  requested=8000   actual=8,000
  unigram  requested=16000  actual=16,000
  unigram  requested=24000  actual=20,832   (corpus supports only 20,832)
  unigram  requested=32000  actual=20,832   (corpus supports only 20,832)
  bpe      requested=1000   actual=1,000
  bpe      requested=2000   actual=2,000
  bpe      requested=4000   actual=4,000
  bpe      requested=8000   actual=8,000
  bpe      requested=16000  actual=16,000
  bpe      requested=24000  actual=24,000
  bpe      requested=32000  actual=32,000

If 'actual' is capped below 'requested', the corpus does not contain enough
distinct subword candidates for that size. Two requested sizes that cap to the
same actual size are THE SAME MODEL and are de-duplicated in section 7.

========================================================================
4. DOES AN UNSEEN WORD'S ROOT APPEAR IN TRAINING WORDS?   <-- THE METRIC
========================================================================
For each unseen test word, take its pieces of at least 3 characters -
the stem, not a grammatical ending. Then ask: does that stem also occur as a
piece inside some word we DID see in training?

If yes, the model can transfer what it learned about the training word to this
brand-new form. That is the entire mechanism of this component.

Pieces shorter than 3 characters are ignored, because single characters
connect everything to everything and mean nothing. That is exactly what made our
first attempt at this measurement saturate at 99.9%.

type        vocab  root shared  no usable root
-----------------------------------------------
unigram     1,000       44.7%          55.2%
unigram     2,000       54.3%          45.5%
unigram     4,000       62.1%          37.8%
unigram     8,000       69.4%          30.5%
unigram    16,000       78.9%          20.9%
unigram    24,000       82.7%          16.5%
unigram    32,000       82.7%          16.5%
bpe         1,000       45.0%          54.8%
bpe         2,000       56.4%          43.4%
bpe         4,000       66.9%          32.9%
bpe         8,000       74.9%          24.8%
bpe        16,000       78.5%          20.3%
bpe        24,000       79.8%          17.7%
bpe        32,000       75.0%          17.6%

'root shared'    = the unseen word contains a >=3-char piece that also occurs
                   inside training words. MAXIMISE THIS.
'no usable root' = the word shattered into pieces all shorter than 3 chars.
                   That is shredding, not decomposition.

========================================================================
4b. GRAPHEME INTEGRITY - are we cutting Sinhala letters in half?
========================================================================
Sinhala writes a syllable as a base consonant plus combining marks - vowel
signs, and the hal kirima. Those are separate Unicode codepoints but NOT
separate letters:

    සෙ  is ONE written letter =  ස (base) + ෙ (combining vowel sign)

If a piece STARTS with a combining mark, the split cut a written letter in half.
Such a piece cannot correspond to any morpheme. This is a Sinhala-specific
failure mode that a pieces-per-word count does not reveal.

----------------------------------------------------------------

unigram     2,000         12.5%          38.9%          34.6%
unigram     4,000         12.0%          35.2%          25.9%
unigram     8,000         11.5%          31.7%          19.3%
unigram    16,000         11.4%          28.4%          11.5%
unigram    24,000         11.0%          25.7%           8.5%
unigram    32,000         11.0%          25.7%           8.5%
bpe         1,000         13.9%          43.7%          42.5%
bpe         2,000         12.0%          35.6%          29.4%
bpe         4,000         10.2%          28.6%          20.3%
bpe         8,000          8.8%          23.2%          14.4%
bpe        16,000          8.0%          19.8%          11.3%
bpe        24,000          7.9%          18.7%           9.7%
bpe        32,000          7.7%          18.3%           9.6%

'broken pieces'  = pieces that begin with a combining mark, i.e. mid-letter cuts
'unseen wds hit' = unseen words containing at least one such piece
'root<2 letters' = every piece is under 2 VISIBLE letters. Pure shredding.

MINIMISE all three. A setting with high root-sharing but many broken graphemes
is sharing meaningless fragments, not roots.

'ok?' = pieces per word under 2.5, so we decompose rather than shred.

THERE ARE TWO COMPETING CRITERIA, NOT ONE.

  'root shared' rewards LARGE vocabularies: longer pieces are more likely to
     match something inside a training word.
  'broken'      often favours a DIFFERENT algorithm: a tokenizer can score well
     on root-sharing while cutting Sinhala letters in half, and those pieces
     cannot correspond to any morpheme.

Do not rank on one column. Compare the algorithms at similar vocabulary sizes.

ALSO CHECK FOR A DEGENERATE WIN. If the best setting keeps 85% of words whole,
the subword channel is nearly a copy of the word channel for words we already
handle, and every gain must come from the unseen words alone. That may still be
worth it, but it is a different mechanism from real decomposition - and it
bounds how much this component can possibly buy us.

DESIGN THE SHORTLIST TO SPAN THE RANGE, NOT TO CLUSTER AT ONE END.
Three near-identical large settings teach us almost nothing. Pick settings that
answer different questions, then let VALIDATION F1 decide.

AUTO-RANKED (by root sharing only - read the caveats above):
  1. unigram, 20,832 pieces  (root shared 82.7%, 1.18 pcs/word, 11.0% broken)
  2. bpe, 24,000 pieces  (root shared 79.8%, 1.17 pcs/word, 7.9% broken)
  3. unigram, 16,000 pieces  (root shared 78.9%, 1.21 pcs/word, 11.4% broken)

  The largest unigram sizes capped at the corpus limit, so that end of the
  curve is fully explored. No need to sweep higher for unigram.

  NOTE: the top setting averages only 1.18 pieces per word, meaning most
  words are kept whole. Include a MORE FRAGMENTING setting in the experiments
  as a contrast, or you will not learn whether decomposition helps at all.
